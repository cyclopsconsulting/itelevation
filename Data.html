Why this program matters

Move from fragmented information to trusted, governed data.

As organizations grow, data is duplicated across systems, defined differently by teams, moved through fragile integrations, and governed inconsistently. Data Management establishes the ownership, standards, quality practices, and integration discipline needed to make information reliable and useful.

Pain point 01

Fragmented data

Critical information is duplicated across systems, making it difficult to establish a consistent view of the business.

Pain point 02

Unclear ownership

Data definitions, stewardship, quality responsibilities, and decision rights may vary across teams.

Pain point 03

Low trust in information

Inconsistent definitions and quality issues increase reconciliation work and weaken reporting confidence.

Pain point 04

Limited analytics readiness

Fragile integrations and unclear lineage make it harder to support automation, analytics, AI, and timely decisions.

Program purpose

Make enterprise data trusted, governed, and usable.

The Data Management program connects business data domains, ownership, standards, quality, integration, and analytics needs into a repeatable capability that improves information trust and supports transformation.

Governance and analytics readiness

Transformation journey

Eight steps from data assessment to trusted, governed information.

The journey can begin with critical data domains and priority reports, then grow into an operating capability that continuously improves data quality, ownership, integration, and analytics readiness.

Assess current state

Review critical reports, data pain points, systems of record, integrations, ownership, quality issues, and analytics needs.

Map data domains

Document critical domains, master and reference data, data flows, reporting dependencies, lineage, and known gaps.

Align stakeholders

Agree on priority domains, definitions, ownership, stewardship, decision rights, and business outcomes.

Design standards

Define data models, common definitions, quality rules, metadata practices, and integration principles.

Prioritize gaps

Rank improvements by business impact, reporting needs, regulatory obligations, risk, and feasibility.

Build the roadmap

Sequence governance, data quality, integration, master data, reporting, and analytics improvements.

Activate governance

Launch stewardship routines, quality reviews, issue resolution, ownership forums, and standards adoption.

Improve continuously

Refresh data models, quality rules, lineage, ownership, and priorities as business needs and systems evolve.

Success measures

Measure trust, visibility, governance, and analytics readiness.

Select measures that reflect critical data domains, information quality needs, reporting dependencies, and business priorities.

Trust

Critical data domains have clear owners, agreed definitions, and quality measures.

Visibility

Data flows, systems of record, reporting dependencies, and lineage are documented and actionable.

Governance & readiness

Data issues are resolved through clear stewardship routines, and analytics initiatives are supported by reliable, accessible information.

Start small, prove value

Start with a focused data assessment.

A focused assessment identifies critical data domains, ownership gaps, quality issues, fragile integrations, and the improvements that will most strengthen reporting and decision-making.

  • Select critical data domains, reports, and business decisions in scope.
  • Map data sources, systems of record, ownership, and key data flows.
  • Assess data quality, definition conflicts, lineage, and governance gaps.
  • Prioritize a small set of quality, integration, and stewardship improvements.
  • Define data owners, success measures, and a practical improvement roadmap.
Typical assessment outputs

A practical data improvement plan

Critical data domain map, ownership and stewardship gaps, quality findings, priority data flows, recommended controls, success measures, and a phased improvement roadmap.
